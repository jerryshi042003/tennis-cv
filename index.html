01

Reference result

Ping-pong: observations become a testable 3D trajectory.

Question. What does a convincing racket-sports reconstruction look like?

Method. Observe the ball, calibrate the scene, fit bounce and flight physics, then reproject the result into the source views.

Why it matters. TT3D exposes the chain and its residuals. It is the right standard for the ball layer, but it does not recover the player, racket, grip, or contact geometry needed for tennis.

02

Synthesis

What actually survived.

Body. Monocular methods can provide a reusable moving body, and calibrated views can validate it. They do not supply tennis-object truth.

Ball. Point tracking is strong on one clear high-speed clip and independently reproducible there. Edge cases and new footage still break it.

Racket. Detection works more often than clean segmentation or continuous pose. Even human-perfect first-frame masks did not propagate reliably.

Next input. The durable route is synchronized, calibrated, high-frame-rate owned capture with a marked racket and known scale—not another model swap on the same broadcast video.

03

Experiment ledger

Every distinct attempt, without repeating the same failure.

Body recovery and reusable motion

Can ordinary or calibrated video produce a body artifact that remains useful outside the source camera?

  1. MediaPipe Pose

    bounded result

    Question: Is cheap 2D pose enough for serve timing and gross arm motion?

    Observed: Ran across tennis clips. Fast and useful for 2D arm diagnostics, but depth, feet, hands, racket, ball, and world motion were not reliable.

    Decision: Keep as a capture and phase diagnostic; never call it a 3D serve reconstruction.

  2. RTMPose-m head-to-head

    kept

    Question: Would a newer 2D pose model materially improve the retained MediaPipe layer?

    Observed: On the same clip, MediaPipe ran at 104 fps versus 4.81 fps and had 59% lower wrist-jerk noise; wrist-speed correlation was 0.895 and both found the same contact frame.

    Decision: The practical 2D layer was not the blocker. Retain MediaPipe rather than swapping models for novelty.

  3. MediaPipe Heavy + world landmarks

    bounded result

    Question: Could the discarded world-landmark output recover axial sequencing?

    Observed: Arm-chain peaks reproduced within one frame, but the axial channels failed the observability and full-versus-heavy agreement gates and were emitted as null.

    Decision: Arm timing survives; monocular axial rotation does not.

  4. GEM-X / SOMA on CalTennis

    bounded result

    Question: Can one public monocular serve become a stable full-body moving mesh?

    Observed: Produced a 71-frame fixed-topology body and orbitable GLB with body and hands.

    Decision: Best executed monocular body baseline; still not grip, racket, ball, contact, or metric depth truth.

  5. GEM-X / SOMA on Djokovic and Medvedev

    bounded result

    Question: Does the monocular body route generalize beyond one CalTennis clip?

    Observed: Produced two additional moving body outputs. Identity stayed coherent enough to inspect, while sport-object layers remained absent.

    Decision: The body route repeats; the missing racket/ball geometry repeats too.

  6. Calibrated X2/X4 body reprojection

    kept

    Question: Is the plausible monocular body spatially consistent in a synchronized second view?

    Observed: Body reached 3.01 px median reprojection across 2,184 observations.

    Decision: The body layer passed its spatial test. Additional body compute was no longer the highest-value next step.

  7. AIMoCap built-in tennis sample

    bounded result

    Question: Can a hosted route quickly return an editable tennis rig?

    Observed: The provided sample yielded a 205-frame, 63-bone FBX. A custom Jerry-source reconstruction was never proved.

    Decision: Useful production reference, not evidence about our footage.

  8. GVHMR hosted custom inference

    stopped

    Question: Could a world-grounded monocular body challenger replace GEM-X?

    Observed: The hosted custom inference returned HTTP 500 and produced no accepted artifact.

    Decision: No output means no basis to outrank the executed baseline.

  9. SAM-Body4D local setup

    not executed

    Question: Could temporal body repair produce a more stable same-source body?

    Observed: The code path was audited, but gated assets and the CUDA-oriented stack prevented a same-source run.

    Decision: Credible literature candidate, but not an executed result in this project.

  10. Bath 4D_56 marker reconstruction

    kept

    Question: Can a public measured serve replace inference for body and racket motion?

    Observed: Parsed 42 serve files. Selected 4D_56 has 301/301 finite frames at 180 Hz; three racket-marker candidates remain rigid within 20 mm throughout.

    Decision: Strongest measured body+racket surrogate; it lacks raw video, camera calibration, ball, impact, and grip truth.

    Open evidence cut ↗

  11. Bath marker-to-skeleton mapping

    stopped

    Question: Could the anonymous marker array become a fully articulated athlete automatically?

    Observed: The release omitted marker names and the available mapping stopped at a proxy marker map rather than a defensible full skeleton.

    Decision: Measured trajectories survive; anatomical labels and articulation require new evidence.

  12. Bath skinned retarget

    stopped

    Question: Can the measured marker motion drive a standard skinned athlete without distortion?

    Observed: The local-rotation retarget missed the wrists by 119.2 mm and 89.6 mm median and was rejected.

    Decision: Use the measured marker truth directly; the stylized player is presentation, not recovered anatomy.

  13. Bath Serve Study presentation

    kept

    Question: Can measured motion still become a watchable teaching artifact after retarget failure?

    Observed: A 118-frame toss→contact→flight→bounce sequence shipped in rear, side, and orbit views with the stylized fallback explicitly labeled.

    Decision: Presentation can clarify measured timing without upgrading the body or ball to measurement.

    Open evidence cut ↗

Racket, ball, grip, and contact

Can the small fast objects be recovered independently instead of being attached to a plausible body?

  1. RacketVision five-point racket fit

    stopped

    Question: Can five racket keypoints yield a rigid racket pose on CalTennis?

    Observed: The synchronized fit reached 17.59 px median reprojection and 23.53 px at contact, failing the acceptance gate.

    Decision: Keypoints are proposals, not an accepted racket SE(3) track.

  2. BallTrack at published threshold

    stopped

    Question: Can the tennis-specific tracker recover the ball through contact?

    Observed: Found only 3 of 131 frames on the first clip and missed contact.

    Decision: The released tennis-specific route did not solve this source.

  3. BallTrack low-threshold retry

    stopped

    Question: Was the failure only a confidence cutoff?

    Observed: Lowering the threshold added static false positives without recovering trustworthy contact evidence.

    Decision: Threshold tuning could not substitute for better observations.

  4. DexGraspNet + MANO handle fitting

    stopped

    Question: Can a generic grasp prior create a credible tennis grip?

    Observed: Optimization improved numeric scores while rendered hands penetrated the handle, opened, or left gaps.

    Decision: A plausible prior is not athlete-specific grip measurement.

  5. Alcaraz manual evidence composite

    kept

    Question: What can careful source-camera annotation establish without pretending to solve 3D?

    Observed: Produced 61 reviewed arm frames, 54 racket silhouettes, observed post-contact ball positions, and one fixed-camera teaching composite.

    Decision: Useful 2D teaching evidence; not a reusable any-angle serve.

  6. Constant hand-socket-to-racket transform

    stopped

    Question: Can separately plausible body and racket layers be made coherent with one rigid attachment?

    Observed: Missed by 207.46 mm median position and 82.25° median orientation.

    Decision: The composed layers were structurally incompatible.

  7. Racket-first authored-grip fit

    stopped

    Question: If racket motion stays fixed, can one local wrist point explain 61 observed wrist positions?

    Observed: Only 7/61 frames met the ≤8 px gate; median error was 15.45 px and max was 57.79 px.

    Decision: Do not export or tune another authored grip from the same evidence.

  8. Wrist-first flipped-anchor fit

    stopped

    Question: If the arm is exact, can the racket follow without breaking continuity?

    Observed: Arm clicks were exact, but arm steps exceeded 20°, racket error reached 46.40 px median, and two face-normal flips remained.

    Decision: The opposite hierarchy failed too; a third monocular composition was closed.

  9. TrackNetV4 / contact-ball attempt

    stopped

    Question: Can a tennis ball detector establish the impact transition on the Alcaraz window?

    Observed: The output could not support a single-frame physical-impact claim; the final page retained only observed 2D positions and a contact bracket.

    Decision: Contact remains a bracket, not a detected instant.

  10. Four-stage synthetic contact lab

    kept

    Question: Can compression, felt, stringbed, and coupled impact be modeled transparently?

    Observed: Passed 37 numerical checks and four runtime invariants on declared synthetic parameters.

    Decision: Useful mechanics sandbox; it explains mechanisms but identifies no real stroke until parameters are measured.

Source quality and capture geometry

Would a better existing clip, more compute, or a different capture contract remove the missing evidence?

  1. 15 local source-window screen

    stopped

    Question: Does any available tennis window preserve cadence, racket size, contact, and useful view together?

    Observed: 0/15 passed all four frozen gates; none reached the ≥80% unique-adjacent-frame cadence requirement.

    Decision: Another convenient internet clip was not the answer.

  2. Seven external high-speed candidates

    stopped

    Question: Do stronger named sources unlock a full serve or contact exhibit?

    Observed: All 4,272 delivered frames were reviewed; 0/7 passed the full-serve profile and 0/7 passed the contact-exhibit profile.

    Decision: High-speed branding did not guarantee recoverable cadence or geometry.

  3. More-body-compute fork

    stopped

    Question: Would renting more GPU fix the current failure?

    Observed: Closed because body reprojection already passed while racket face, palm-handle relation, and contact evidence remained absent.

    Decision: Compute cannot invent unrecorded geometry.

  4. Owned-capture intake validator

    not executed

    Question: Could synchronized 240 fps rear/side footage be accepted before expensive inference?

    Observed: Built fail-closed checks for native media, synchronization, calibration, marker visibility, rights, and evidence windows; no qualifying owned capture existed.

    Decision: The required next input is explicit and testable, not a vague request for better video.

  5. Synthetic self-calibration test

    stopped

    Question: Can skeleton geometry remove the need for a known calibration object?

    Observed: At 1–3 px pose noise, recovered camera pose was off by 6–8°, about one metre lateral error at seven metres.

    Decision: Retain a known-length object in both views; self-calibration was not obviously safe.

  6. Salvage test suite

    kept

    Question: Should imperfect footage be discarded or still analyzed?

    Observed: Three of five documented failures stayed analyzable. Cropping the racket and severe backlighting were the decisive losses; 120 fps, sub-1080p, portrait, dim light, and split takes were not automatic discards.

    Decision: Send flawed footage and annotate the flaw; do not destroy evidence by reflexively reshooting.

  7. Gravity-clock recovery

    stopped

    Question: Can unknown slow-motion playback rate be inferred from the corpus after capture?

    Observed: Two independent recovery routes failed; physics fitting needs the clock that the internet copies no longer preserve.

    Decision: Any speed, duration, sequence timing, or physics prior needs verified native frame timing.

  8. Toss detector escalation

    stopped

    Question: Can three automatic ball-detection methods recover toss apexes?

    Observed: Validation collapsed from 1/7 to 0/4 to 0/1; the top-scoring detection was a ceiling light. The registered result was void.

    Decision: Eye-and-grid became the instrument; automated detection did not earn the measurement.

  9. Manual toss-apex adjudication

    bounded result

    Question: What clockless serve quantity can the old corpus still answer?

    Observed: For seven adjudicated kick serves, 4/7 apexes were on the non-hitting side and median x was −0.232 torso lengths; the slice comparison lacked enough valid rows.

    Decision: A small measured descriptive result survives; the registered kick-vs-slice comparison does not.

Simulation, controllers, and generated presentation

Can physics or generation make the result useful without being confused with observation?

  1. Authored ball-flight simulator

    kept

    Question: Can a declared physics ball provide a coherent teaching layer?

    Observed: Passed its teaching gates at 6.725 px median / 19.234 px p95 reprojection, 0.0511 m net clearance, and an in-box first bounce.

    Decision: Useful as authored physics; launch and spin remain unmeasured.

  2. RIFE interpolation

    kept

    Question: Can sparse presentation frames be made easier to watch?

    Observed: Produced 13/13 deterministic midpoints in 19 seconds, continuously labeled as interpolated and not captured.

    Decision: Presentation-only pixels stay outside evidence receipts.

  3. SONIC controller

    stopped

    Question: Can a released physics controller run locally on the Mac?

    Observed: Environment and assets loaded, but the Linux/CUDA/TensorRT controller produced zero controlled steps.

    Decision: Dead on this machine; do not treat setup success as a motion result.

  4. ProtoMotions CPU policy

    bounded result

    Question: Can an open physics policy execute locally and accept the recovered athlete later?

    Observed: Ran 19,801 MuJoCo steps across 58 motions with 0 invalid states and 0.965517 success; the Alcaraz target stopped because its GLB had no skin, bones, or joint rotations.

    Decision: Controller works; articulation transport is the wall.

  5. DeepMimic Apple-CPU port

    stopped

    Question: Can the older controller provide a second local feasibility check?

    Observed: Engine, checkpoint, and renderer ran; one diagnostic stayed finite, but the five-minute repeat diverged with 3,715 non-finite markers.

    Decision: Not stable enough and not tennis-specific; no training run justified.

  6. TrajectoryCrafter / cloud generation

    stopped

    Question: Can a generative video route supply missing camera or motion evidence?

    Observed: Quota and propagation tests did not produce an evidence-safe, source-faithful reconstruction.

    Decision: Generated views cannot become measurements.

  7. LTX-Video local path

    stopped

    Question: Can local video generation run inside the available Apple-memory budget?

    Observed: The PyTorch/MPS route exceeded the practical memory envelope and was stopped.

    Decision: Framework and memory wall; no evidence artifact.

  8. SeedVR2 3B restoration

    stopped

    Question: Can local restoration clarify a 6000 fps contact reference without changing evidence?

    Observed: Ran in 6.44 seconds at 4.05 GB peak memory, sharpened the frame, and invented ball-seam texture.

    Decision: Useful only for presentation; rejected as evidence.

Ball and racket tracking progression

A controlled sequence of same-source tests: change one observation or tracking primitive, inspect every output, then pivot only from the failure.

  1. Three-clip released-model bakeoff

    stopped

    Question: Would clearer real sources make BallTrack, RacketPose, MediaPipe, and hand tracking work unchanged?

    Observed: Only 1/15 sources passed the initial readability gate. Ball and racket models still failed; MediaPipe passed gross 2D arm alignment on the clearest clip only.

    Decision: Source quality helped body pose but did not rescue the small-object models.

  2. CoTracker3 Offline ball tracking

    kept

    Question: Can a generic point tracker retain ball center through racket overlap?

    Observed: Passed on the 76-frame clear clip: 54/57 verified ball frames, 0.99 px median, 2.68 px p90, 1.87 px at overlap, and zero false points after exit.

    Decision: Strongest surviving source-camera ball observation on a clear clip.

    Open evidence cut ↗

  3. SAM 2.1 Tiny racket silhouette

    kept

    Question: Can a generic segmenter retain the racket as a 2D mask?

    Observed: Stayed attached for 76/76 frames on the clear clip; its ball mask failed the center gate.

    Decision: Useful 2D silhouette on readable inputs; not rigid pose or general tracking.

    Open evidence cut ↗

  4. CoTracker3 replication on two harder clips

    stopped

    Question: Does the ball result survive occlusion, clipping, and repeated slow motion?

    Observed: Failed one clipped/overlap case and reached only 41/55 attached on the repeated-slow-motion case.

    Decision: The clear-clip win did not establish general ball tracking.

  5. SAM 2 replication on two harder clips

    stopped

    Question: Does the racket silhouette survive different backgrounds and scale?

    Observed: One clip failed because the seed included the ball; one repeated clip stayed attached but did not establish new-frame continuity.

    Decision: Seed purity and source cadence dominate downstream memory.

  6. SAM 2 prompt ablation

    stopped

    Question: Would box, point, or mixed prompts turn SAM 2 into a general racket tracker?

    Observed: All prompt types ended at the same 3/5 clips; wall/background and tiny-racket failures persisted.

    Decision: Prompt tuning was closed as the next lever.

  7. Cutie memory tracker

    stopped

    Question: Would a materially different memory model repair SAM 2 failures?

    Observed: Also finished at 3/5 clips. It removed one detached line but preserved background already included in the initial mask.

    Decision: A different tracker cannot repair a wrong observation.

  8. TAPNext++ ball replication

    kept

    Question: Can an independent point tracker reproduce the ball result?

    Observed: Passed the clear clip at 55/57 within 8 px, 1.56 px median and 4.77 px p90; improved a repeated clip but failed the clipped edge case.

    Decision: The clear-clip ball result is independently reproducible, not general.

  9. Grounding DINO racket discovery

    kept

    Question: Can open-vocabulary detection at least locate the real racket automatically?

    Observed: Found the actual racket in all 5/5 frozen observations, including the prior wall-prompt failure.

    Decision: Keep the detector as a proposal stage.

  10. Box-only SAM 2 seed

    stopped

    Question: Is a correct detector box sufficient for a clean racket mask?

    Observed: Passed only 1/5; hand, wrist, ball, spectator, and background were absorbed.

    Decision: Detection and instance separation are different problems.

  11. YOLOE instance separation

    bounded result

    Question: Can open-vocabulary segmentation produce a cleaner initial racket mask?

    Observed: Two of five masks stayed clean for 177/177 frames; three fused with hand, body, or background.

    Decision: A clean seed propagates; generic masks are not reliably clean.

  12. RacketVision + YOLOE + SAM 2 hybrid

    stopped

    Question: Can a fixed gate choose the better automatic seed per clip?

    Observed: Selected RacketVision on 3/5 and YOLOE on 2/5, but still passed only 2/5 clips and 177/415 frames—the same as YOLOE alone.

    Decision: More routing logic added no net evidence.

  13. Official RacketVision validation

    kept

    Question: Does the released checkpoint work in its own tennis domain?

    Observed: Detected 28/28 official validation rackets with 0.845 median box IoU and 84.96% PCK@0.1.

    Decision: The release itself works in-domain.

  14. RacketVision on five external observations

    stopped

    Question: Does that in-domain quality transfer to our frozen sources?

    Observed: Produced coherent five-point geometry on only 3/5 observations; two collapsed or displaced.

    Decision: External-style racket data, not another threshold, is the missing lever.

  15. Box-only pseudo-label pilot

    stopped

    Question: Can independent automatic teachers create training masks without hand labels?

    Observed: Produced 0/48 strict-clean racket masks.

    Decision: No training was allowed from contaminated labels.

  16. Racket-keypoints + SAM 2 pseudo-label pilot

    stopped

    Question: Do released racket keypoints improve the mask corpus enough to train?

    Observed: Improved to 18/48 strict-clean, but tiny rackets were 0/12 and the 36/48 training gate failed.

    Decision: Automatic pseudo-label training remained unsafe.

  17. SAM 2.1 Tiny versus Large

    stopped

    Question: Was model capacity the cause of the failed masks?

    Observed: Both finished at exactly 18/48 clean; Large was 4.302× slower and the first full-process run died after 30/48.

    Decision: Bigger segmentation compute changed zero verdicts.

  18. Structured racket-shaped seed

    stopped

    Question: Would an ellipse head plus shaft prompt preserve the complete racket?

    Observed: Passed 0/248 frames and 0/8 clips; SAM rewrote the seed toward the stringbed and lost shaft/handle.

    Decision: Prompt geometry was closed.

  19. Human-complete polygon seed

    stopped

    Question: Would one correct full racket silhouette unlock temporal propagation?

    Observed: Eight accepted human masks still produced 0/240 strict-clean held-out frames and 0/8 passing clips.

    Decision: One-frame correction is insufficient; dense labels or marked capture are next.

    Open evidence cut ↗

  20. Independent per-frame RacketVision

    stopped

    Question: Can temporal failure be avoided by reconstructing each frame independently?

    Observed: Detected 248/248, passed automatic topology on 118/248, and human inspection accepted 75/248; 0/8 clips passed continuity.

    Decision: Useful proposal frames, not a continuous racket layer.

    Open evidence cut ↗

  21. Rigid 2D racket composition

    bounded result

    Question: Can accepted per-frame anchors become one watchable constant-shape racket with only bounded interpolation?

    Observed: All 29 frames passed visual continuity, but anchor p95 was 16.136 px against a frozen 15 px gate.

    Decision: Useful authored teaching overlay; one measurable fidelity gate still failed.

    Open evidence cut ↗

Corpus, metrics, and reversals

Tests that changed what could be claimed, even when they did not produce a prettier demo.

  1. Large public-source harvest

    bounded result

    Question: Can scale solve the footage problem?

    Observed: 1,424 candidates → 379 screened → 98 curated and downloaded; only 41.9% of the evaluated pose subset supported any defensible quantity.

    Decision: Harvesting more internet video multiplies unusable footage; controlled capture has higher leverage.

  2. SigLIP 2 semantic frame retrieval

    kept

    Question: Can review scale without replacing human evidence checks?

    Observed: Embedded 1,568 frames and found the correct frame for all four initial text queries at 129 frames/s on MPS.

    Decision: Keep as retrieval and triage, never as a measurement oracle.

  3. Proximal-to-distal ordering test

    stopped

    Question: Can broadcast footage recover the serve sequence?

    Observed: Ordering held in 29.6% of 27 gated reps, at or below chance.

    Decision: The headline biomechanics claim was retired for this corpus.

  4. Peak-failure re-slice

    corrected

    Question: Was frame quantization alone causing the ordering failure?

    Observed: Only 22% of reps had truly sub-frame lag; large errors mostly came from choosing different noisy peaks, not merely low frame rate.

    Decision: The negative result survived, but its causal explanation was corrected.

  5. Torso-size usability gate

    corrected

    Question: Does torso ≥90 px identify usable clips?

    Observed: Misclassified 37.2% of 86 clips and was removed as a hard abort.

    Decision: A convenient gate was not load-bearing.

  6. Playback-rate contamination test

    stopped

    Question: Did spatial normalization remove slow-motion speed differences?

    Observed: Normalized speed had Spearman rho = −1.00 with playback slowdown.

    Decision: Every speed, duration, and rate claim dies without the native clock.

  7. Axial-observability claim audit

    corrected

    Question: Was axial rotation truly 0% observable?

    Observed: No. The zero came from a gating bug; remeasurement found 89.8% finite observability within ±2 s, while the values still failed model-agreement gates.

    Decision: The 0% headline was retracted; the weaker conclusion—monocular axial estimates are unstable—remains.

  8. Kovacs law adjudicability census

    kept

    Question: How much coaching doctrine can the internet corpus actually test?

    Observed: 0/11 laws admitted a fresh test on the old corpus; five become testable with controlled capture, three need more, and three are not video questions.

    Decision: Use doctrine as a measurement agenda, not as evidence for its own claims.

  9. Evidence-state visual grammar

    kept

    Question: Can measured, computed, inferred, interpreted, and source claims remain distinguishable at scale?

    Observed: The grammar was tested across 41 cards, contrast failures and vocabulary collisions were corrected, and tamper tests caught drift.

    Decision: Evidence origin, observability, verdict, and release verification must remain separate dimensions.

04

Selected literature

Methods that define the ceiling or the missing layer.

  1. TT3D ↗

    Table-tennis ball, scene calibration, bounce physics, and spin. Best visible proof that a physics fit plus reprojection can turn sparse ball observations into a coherent 3D rally. It does not solve player or racket pose.

  2. Uplifting Table Tennis ↗

    Noisy 2D ball observations → physically credible 3D. Best ball-layer QA blueprint: detection, filtering, lifting, physics, and reprojection shown separately.

  3. Vid2Player3D ↗

    Reusable physics-trained tennis player. Best long-term player/controller precedent; trained weights are withheld and close grip/contact truth is not proved.

  4. GEM-X / GENMO ↗

    Expressive monocular body recovery. Current executed body baseline; reusable body sequence, no independent racket or ball.

  5. SOMA-X ↗

    Identity/motion representation and retargeting. Useful infrastructure for persistent bodies; compatibility does not validate source motion.

  6. MAMMA ↗

    Calibrated multi-view body reconstruction. Best open owned-capture body ceiling; still requires independent racket and ball layers.

  7. FoundationPose ↗

    Novel rigid-object 6D pose. Correct artifact class for a racket, but thin fast symmetric rackets need markers, CAD, depth/calibration, and contact checks.

  8. Open4DHOI ↗

    Monocular human–object reconstruction. The closest research framing for hand/racket coupling; sparse contact annotation remains necessary.

  9. RacketVision ↗

    Racket-sport ball and articulated racket benchmark. Most relevant benchmark lineage; in-domain validation did not transfer cleanly to all external observations.

  10. BlurBall ↗

    Fast blurred-ball observations. Treats blur as motion evidence rather than detector failure; front end only, not 3D or spin.

  11. SAM-Body4D ↗

    Temporally repaired body meshes. Strong same-source body challenger once gated assets and CUDA are available; body only.

  12. WHAM / GVHMR ↗

    World-grounded monocular body motion. Important root/world-motion lineage; neither removes racket, ball, grip, or contact ambiguity.
